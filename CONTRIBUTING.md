# Contributing to Awesome Digital Tools

Thank you for your interest in contributing to **Awesome Digital Tools**!

Awesome Digital Tools is a curated collection of popular, widely used, and genuinely useful digital tools. Our goal is to maintain a high-quality, practical resource—not to list every tool that exists.

Please read these guidelines before opening a pull request (PR).

## Acceptance Criteria

Every proposed entry should meet the following criteria:

- **Popularity and adoption:** The tool should have demonstrated adoption, an established user base, meaningful community activity, credible industry usage, or other reliable evidence that people use it.
- **Practical value:** The tool must solve a clear problem or provide a useful capability to its intended audience.
- **Relevance:** The tool must fit the repository's scope and an existing category. If no category fits, explain why a new category is necessary.
- **Official link:** Include a working official website or an official project page. Do not submit tracking links, referral links, or unrelated landing pages.
- **Quality and credibility:** The tool should have a clear purpose and sufficient documentation. Its developer or organization should be reasonably identifiable.
- **Accurate description:** Provide a concise, factual description of what the tool does. Avoid promotional language, unsupported claims, and subjective superlatives.
- **No duplicates:** Check the list before submitting. Do not add an entry that is already listed under another name.
- **Current availability:** The tool should be maintained and accessible. If it is no longer available or has been abandoned, explain why it should still be included.

## Evidence of Popularity and Use

Popularity is not determined by GitHub stars alone. Different types of tools have different signals of adoption.

When relevant, use credible evidence such as:

- An established user or customer base, or published adoption figures.
- Active community participation or a substantial user community.
- Credible industry use, independent coverage, or inclusion in reputable collections.
- Meaningful project activity, releases, documentation, and user feedback.
- Recognition within the tool's specific field or audience.

Include a brief explanation or source links in your PR when the tool's adoption may not be obvious. Do not invent metrics or treat a single popularity signal as conclusive.

New or niche tools are not automatically disqualified, but they need a clear reason for inclusion and credible evidence of usefulness or adoption.

## What Is Unlikely to Be Accepted

A PR may be declined if it adds:

- Tools with little evidence of real-world use, usefulness, or adoption.
- Newly launched or experimental tools without enough evidence to assess their value.
- Abandoned, broken, inaccessible, or misleading projects.
- Tools unrelated to the repository's scope.
- Duplicate tools or entries that add little distinct value to the list.
- Entries with unofficial, incorrect, dead, referral, or tracking links.
- Vague, inaccurate, exaggerated, or promotional descriptions.
- A new category that is unnecessary, too narrow, or unsupported by enough quality entries.
- Changes that do not follow the repository's formatting or contribution requirements.

These are review guidelines, not an automatic rejection formula. Each submission is evaluated on its own merits.

## Before Opening a Pull Request

Please check that:

1. The tool is not already listed.
2. The official link works and points to the correct product or project.
3. The tool fits the repository's purpose and the selected category.
4. The description is concise, neutral, and accurate.
5. You have included relevant evidence of adoption or practical value, especially for less-known tools.
6. Your changes follow the existing file format, ordering, and style.
7. You have checked your changes for spelling, broken links, duplicates, and formatting errors.

Please keep each PR focused on a small, related set of changes. Explain what you added or changed and why the entry belongs in this collection.

## Review and Maintainer Decisions

All submissions are reviewed at the maintainers' discretion. Meeting the criteria does not guarantee acceptance.

Maintainers may request more information, ask for revisions, or close a PR when an entry does not meet the repository's scope or quality standards. A lack of sufficient evidence may be a reason to decline a submission; it is not a judgment of the contributor.

The project's priority is **quality, relevance, demonstrated usefulness, and adoption—not the number of entries**.

## Questions

If you are unsure whether a tool qualifies, open an issue before submitting a PR and briefly describe the tool, its intended audience, and any evidence of adoption or usefulness.

Thank you for helping keep **Awesome Digital Tools** useful, trustworthy, and well curated!
